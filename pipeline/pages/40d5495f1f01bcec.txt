Dundee Rep and Scottish Dance Theatre announce 2027 season including Stanley Baxter play
The venue has three pieces of new writing across next year
Alex Wood
|
Dundee
|
17 November 2026
Artwork for Huntly, photo supplied by Dundee Rep
Dundee Rep and Scottish Dance Theatre have announced their 2027 season.
The season opens with The Ferry, written by Linda McLean from an original idea by Paul Rattray and directed by Jemima Levick. A co-production between Dundee Rep Theatre, Tron Theatre, Traverse Theatre and English Touring Theatre, the play follows 15-year-old Freddy, who travels from Glasgow to Dundee after becoming orphaned. It makes its world premiere at Dundee Rep from 6 to 20 February 2027, before visiting Tron Theatre in Glasgow from 23 February to 6 March and the Traverse Theatre in Edinburgh from 9 to 20 March.
In May, Stanley will receive its world premiere. Written by Stanley Baxter’s biographer Brian Beacom and directed by Johnny McKnight, the play charts the entertainer’s career, from his early performances in Glasgow to his success as one of Britain’s best-known entertainers. It also explores his private life and the difficulties he faced behind his many onstage characters.
Presented in association with Citizens Theatre, Stanley runs at Dundee Rep from 8 to 22 May, before visiting King’s Theatre, Edinburgh (1 to 5 June), Citizens Theatre, Glasgow (9 to 12 June) and Pitlochry Festival Theatre (15 to 19 June).
August sees the arrival of Huntly, co-created and written by Lesley Hart and Denise Mina, based on Mina’s novella Rizzio and directed by Sally Reid. Set in 1566, the play follows the murder of David Rizzio, close advisor to Mary, Queen of Scots, by Scottish nobles seeking power. With the pregnant Queen held hostage and her allies turning against her, the story centres on Huntly, a woman who must decide whether to help Mary despite their differences.
Presented by Dundee Rep and Traverse Theatre, Huntly forms part of TravFest27 at the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, running at the Traverse Theatre from 1 to 29 August before transferring to Dundee Rep from 31 August to 4 September.
Scottish Dance Theatre’s 2027 programme includes the world premiere of The Witches’ Hour, created by artistic director Joan Clevillé, in a double bill with Rotten Work by choreographer Emilie Leriche. Inspired by Scotland’s history of witchcraft and its association with the supernatural, the work explores difference and identity. The double bill previews at Dundee Rep on 25 and 26 June before transferring to the Edinburgh Festival Fringe.
The company will also revive Pirates!, choreographed by Clevillé, which combines dance, theatre and storytelling with original music by Luke Sutherland. The production runs at Dundee Rep from 2 to 3 April, before visiting King’s Theatre Edinburgh (21 and 22 May) and The Place in London (28 and 29 May).
For younger audiences, Little Ray, created with early years arts organisation Starcatchers, is aimed at children aged three to five and explores emotions through movement, music and interaction. It runs at Dundee Rep from 13 to 15 May.
Musical theatre features in the programme with a new production of the previously-announced Guys & Dolls, running from 8 to 19 June. The production brings together Dundee Rep Theatre, Royal Lyceum Theatre Edinburgh, Aberdeen Performing Arts, Eden Court Inverness and Citizens Theatre.
Dundee Rep Theatre, Royal Lyceum Theatre Edinburgh and Citizens Theatre will also reunite for an autumn co-production, with further details to be announced.
At Christmas, the venue will revive A Christmas Carol, its musical adaptation of Charles Dickens’ novel, created by Noisemaker, the writing and composing partnership of Scott Gilmour and Claire McKenzie (responsible for hit musical Ceilidh), and directed by Andrew Panton. The production previously ran in 2021 and 2023 and returns from 27 November to 30 December 2027.
Beyond Scotland, Dundee Rep will see its musical A History of Paper transfer to New York for a 13-week Off-Broadway run at Stage 42, part of the Shubert Organization.
Lorraine Kelly and KT Tunstall will join actor Brian Cox as patrons of the organisation. Kelly has links to Dundee, having lived in Broughty Ferry and served as the first female Rector of the University of Dundee. Tunstall previously collaborated with Dundee Rep and Solar Bear, alongside Aberdeen Performing Arts, on the 2026 five-star production The Singer.
Kath Mainland, executive director of Dundee Rep and Scottish Dance Theatre, said: “Our 2027 season celebrates the breadth of creativity at Dundee Rep and Scottish Dance Theatre, alongside our commitment to make the arts accessible to more people. There and Back captures what makes this organisation special – our connection to Dundee, our communities and the people at the heart of everything we do.
“Joining Brian Cox, we are delighted to welcome Lorraine Kelly and KT Tunstall as patrons of the Rep. Both have strong personal ties to Dundee and a deep connection to the city, and we know they’ll play an important role in supporting our mission to tell great stories, rooted in this place.”
Cox added: “Dundee Rep is where my journey began, and no matter how far my career has taken me, coming back here always feels like coming home. Seeing work created in Dundee now reaching audiences around the world is something to be immensely proud of. It’s a reminder of why places like the Rep matter, and why we must protect them so future generations have the same opportunities to begin their own journeys.”
