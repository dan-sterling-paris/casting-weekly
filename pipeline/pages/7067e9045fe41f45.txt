Only Fools and Horses musical to embark on brand new tour – with initial casting confirmed
It’s back!
Alex Wood
|
Tour
|
19 November 2026
Paul Whitehouse, © Johan Persson
Only Fools and Horses The Musical will tour the UK and Ireland in 2027 and 2028. The tour opens at the Churchill Theatre, Bromley, on 10 September 2027 and visits more than 40 cities and towns before it closes in summer 2028.
Paul Whitehouse, who co-wrote the show, will play Grandad again on select dates. Sam Lupton returns as Del Boy and Tom Major returns as Rodney. Further casting will be announced.
The musical is written by Whitehouse and Jim Sullivan, with the backing of the Sullivan estate. It is based on the BBC series Only Fools and Horses by John Sullivan, Jim Sullivan’s father. Whitehouse and Jim Sullivan wrote the original music, and John Sullivan and Chas Hodges wrote additional music. Caroline Jay Ranger, who directed the original West End production, directs again. Stuart Morley is musical supervisor and arranger.
The show is set in Peckham in 1989. Del Boy searches for a partner, Rodney and Cassandra prepare to marry, Trigger has a date, Boycie and Marlene try once more for a baby, and Grandad takes stock of his life. The score has 20 songs, including a new arrangement of the series’ theme tune.
The musical played a four-year run in the West End, giving more than 1,000 performances at the Theatre Royal Haymarket, and has also toured regionally.
Whitehouse said: “We are delighted to announce that Only Fools and Horses the Musical is bounding back to the stage in 2027. From the West End to the regions, we have been overwhelmed by the love and support this brilliant show has received by generations of fans across the UK and Ireland, and we can’t wait to share more love and laughter on this tour. Don’t delay, get on the blower, and get a ticket for a truly cushty night out – only a 42 carat plonker would miss it!”
The tour will visit the Churchill Theatre, Bromley (10 to 11 September 2027), Princess Theatre, Torquay (14 to 18 September), Bristol Hippodrome (21 to 25 September), Swan Theatre, Wycombe (28 September to 2 October), Portsmouth Guildhall (6 to 9 October), Orchard Theatre, Dartford (12 to 16 October), Venue Cymru, Llandudno (19 to 23 October), Grand Opera House, York (26 to 30 October), Birmingham Hippodrome (2 to 6 November), Waterside Theatre, Aylesbury (9 to 13 November), Sunderland Empire (16 to 20 November), Wales Millennium Centre, Cardiff (23 to 27 November), G Live, Guildford (30 November to 4 December), Congress Theatre, Eastbourne (7 to 11 December), Brighton Dome (14 to 18 December) and Royal Concert Hall, Nottingham (28 December 2027 to 2 January 2028).
In 2028, the tour continues at the Palace Theatre, Manchester (4 to 15 January), Connexin Live, Hull (3 to 5 February), Edinburgh Playhouse (8 to 12 February), His Majesty’s Theatre, Aberdeen (15 to 19 February), King’s Theatre, Glasgow (22 to 26 February), New Theatre, Oxford (29 February to 4 March), Cliffs Pavilion, Southend (7 to 18 March), Swansea Building Society Arena (21 to 25 March), Globe, Stockton (28 March to 1 April), Grand Opera House, Belfast (4 to 8 April), Bord Gáis Energy Theatre, Dublin (11 to 15 April), Opera House, Blackpool (18 to 22 April), Grand Theatre, Leeds (25 to 29 April), City Hall, Sheffield (2 to 6 May), Liverpool Empire (9 to 20 May), Mayflower Theatre, Southampton (23 to 27 May), Hall for Cornwall, Truro (30 May to 3 June), Regent Theatre, Ipswich (6 to 10 June), Milton Keynes Theatre (13 to 17 June), De Montfort Hall, Leicester (27 June to 1 July), Marlowe Theatre, Canterbury (4 to 8 July), Theatre Royal, Norwich (11 to 15 July), Theatre Royal, Newcastle (18 to 22 July) and Alhambra Theatre, Bradford (25 to 29 July).
